## A window is composed of the requests the provider accepts

ESPN refuses date ranges (`dates=A-B`, since Oct 2026) and serves one day or one calendar month. A day response states the season of the DATE asked, and each competition turns on its own date (June 1, July 1, January 1, measured), so a window's parts can state two seasons.

## From `.cursor/rules/trust-boundary.mdc`

### Rules

- **A composed window fails as a whole on what no sibling can stand in for.** A caller that composes windows itself (discovery: a window per month) asks "no readable record" of the whole too: a window refused because its list held no readable record carries `ProviderError.noReadableRecord` and read nothing; an envelope nobody could read carries no such mark and still fails it.

## The bullet whole: "A window is composed of the requests the provider accepts"

The `AGENTS.md` "Conventions" bullet as it stood before its narration moved here, word for word (`AGENTS.md` keeps its lead and the sentences an agent holds on every task, each one a sentence or a clause of this text). Where this text and `AGENTS.md` differ, `AGENTS.md` wins.

**A window is composed of the requests the provider accepts.** `EspnAdapter.fetchWindow` asks a day at a time (up to three days) or a month at a time (longer; more than three months is refused unasked), sends the parts together, and settles every one before it answers, so a throttle that arrives after a sibling's quick failure has armed the cooldown. It returns ONE result with one account: any part's failure fails the window (a throttle wins: the one the adapter retains, whose deadline is latest counted from when each was received); a part whose ENVELOPE cannot be read is a failed part, whatever its siblings hold (the unreadable-envelope rule above is asked of each response); a part that filled the request's `limit` (the provider returns a silent chronological prefix) fails it too; a refused RECORD, or a fixture in two parts (compared before a month is narrowed to the window; the first copy counts), leaves it `complete: false` with the readable rest usable, and the "no readable record" rule is asked of the whole window, not of each part. Its account states `seasons` (every distinct season the parts stated, in the order asked; empty is "none stated") and `omitted` (the records that made it not whole: the parser's count per part, a refused record, a duplicate, one beyond the bound, plus each second copy across parts; 0 exactly when `complete`; absent on a single read that filled its limit, whose tail nobody can count). The count is over the RESPONSES the window was composed from, a month response whole: a record refused outside the span counts (it already makes `complete` false, and a refused record often has no readable date), so it bounds what the span may be missing, and every sentence built on it says "may be incomplete". Two season rules, kept two: the WINDOW states the season its stating parts agree on (a part that states none does not veto); DISCOVERY states one only when every month states the same. Two seasons in a window are then a failure when it is asked strictly (the default, and every caller that merges the bundle or keeps a slice: an absent season would let the bundle apply or a slice of another edition stand), and a composed answer with no `season` and both in `seasons` when it is asked `acrossSeasons`, which a caller that merges nothing and keeps no slice does (a refusal there was three requests spent on a verdict nobody can act on): the live read (`getLiveRead`: it keeps the matches in play), and OFF the bundled competition the dated read (`getMatchesForDate`: no skeleton to merge) and the match read (`match <id>` refreshing the one record it found). On the bundle the dated read on a turn day is still refused. A composed season keeps its start and end date only when every stating part (every month, for discovery) stated the same one (`agreedSeason`): agreeing on the year is not agreeing on when the edition ends. A fixture's day is the PROVIDER's (US/Eastern, measured; `bucketDay`), which is what the month filter keeps by and what the canary checks on every dated response. An incomplete answer cannot prove a fixture is gone: a caller that keeps a previous answer (the refresher's knockout slice, and off the bundle its schedule slice) must not let it erase one. It keeps what the answer did not READ (`mentioned`: everything the answer read, also a tie it set aside as postponed or played). The knockout slice does so only while both seasons are known to be the same; with either unknown the slice stands as it was and the read counts as a failed attempt. The schedule slice does so unless the two seasons are known to DIFFER, because there an unknown season is an ordinary state (a discovery whose span touches two seasons states none); only an answer known to be another season replaces it (`applyDiscovery`). The season it then stores is a claim about every entry, so a union that KEPT entries stores the answer's season only when the two were already known to be the same, and none otherwise: a kept entry is never given a season it was not read under (an answer of "another" season could then delete it). The knockout-facing interactive reads (`next`, `bracket`, their share cards; CLI text and `--json`, MCP text and `data`) carry the window's `complete: false` as the `partial` verdict (see the verdict bullet), and so do `next` and `match <id>` off the bundle (from discovery, or from the match's refresh), and every other interactive read: `today`, `live`, `match` on the bundle, the market surfaces (`markets <id>`, `markets <team>` through `marketFixtureForTeam`, `markets <date>`), and their MCP and share twins (see the verdict bullet for what each prints). The statusline, the hook and the cache do not carry it: they are ambient and cache-only; the cache's live slice carries ONE boolean about its read, `liveComplete` (whether the read that filled it was whole), which the statusline's syncing rule and `ambient --json`'s `current` ask, and no other verdict. The snapshot also keeps the refresher's record of play (`play`, `packages/cli/src/play.ts`: the ids it saw in play, and the matches a WHOLE read then returned finished, each with the time first seen so, for 10 minutes), advanced by one pure function (`advancePlay`) under the lock on both publishers, kept as it was across a failed, degraded or partial read, carried on every publish, and never decided by a truncated list: full time is a match seen in play that a whole read returns finished, never a match that left the list; `ambient --json` lists it as `finished`, each entry for its own 10 minutes by the reader's clock while the snapshot's last live read was whole (`liveComplete`), whatever the snapshot's display window; the statusline's countdown and `ambient --json`'s `next` read only its ids (a match the record holds, in play or finished in its last 10 minutes, is never a due fixture's `now`; `playRecordIds`, once per render, without sealing a record), the hook reads none of it, and the hook's line gains `(FT)` for a finished record only (an English token, as the ambient surfaces are). A three-day window cut at the provider's limit still THROWS (it is `degraded`, not `partial`): turning it into a returned partial would change what the refresher does with it.
