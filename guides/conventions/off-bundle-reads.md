## Off the bundled competition, `next`, `match <id>` and `bracket` answer from the competition itself

(AGENTS.md keeps "The end is the provider's STATED season end (`ended`: its calendar day), an administrative date, not the last match:") on the real feed the World Cup's slug states Dec 31 and league seasons follow each other, so the sentence is said where the provider says a season ended and no next one has started.

## A window is composed of the requests the provider accepts

ESPN refuses date ranges (`dates=A-B`, since Oct 2026) and serves one day or one calendar month. A day response states the season of the DATE asked, and each competition turns on its own date (June 1, July 1, January 1, measured), so a window's parts can state two seasons.

## From `.cursor/rules/trust-boundary.mdc`

### Rules

- **A composed window fails as a whole on what no sibling can stand in for.** A caller that composes windows itself (discovery: a window per month) asks "no readable record" of the whole too: a window refused because its list held no readable record carries `ProviderError.noReadableRecord` and read nothing; an envelope nobody could read carries no such mark and still fails it.

## The bullet whole: "A window is composed of the requests the provider accepts"

The `AGENTS.md` "Conventions" bullet in full: its text as it stood on `main` before this section was added, with the clauses the code disproved corrected in place (`AGENTS.md` carries the same corrections wherever it keeps the clause). `AGENTS.md` now keeps its lead and a selection of its sentences and clauses, with a few joining words and a closing sentence of its own. A rule stated only here still holds: a sentence `AGENTS.md` leaves out is not a rule withdrawn. A reference in this text to "above" or to another bullet is to `AGENTS.md`: the bullets before this one there, and for one of the four bullets held whole, its own guide.

**A window is composed of the requests the provider accepts.** `EspnAdapter.fetchWindow` asks a day at a time (up to three days) or a month at a time (longer; more than three months is refused unasked), sends the parts together, and settles every one before it answers, so a throttle that arrives after a sibling's quick failure has armed the cooldown. It returns ONE result with one account: any part's failure fails the window (a throttle wins: the one the adapter retains, whose deadline is latest counted from when each was received); a part whose ENVELOPE cannot be read is a failed part, whatever its siblings hold (the unreadable-envelope rule above is asked of each response); a part that filled the request's `limit` (the provider returns a silent chronological prefix) fails it too; a refused RECORD, or a fixture in two parts (compared before a month is narrowed to the window; the first copy counts), leaves it `complete: false` with the readable rest usable, and the "no readable record" rule is asked of the whole window, not of each part. Its account states `seasons` (every distinct season the parts stated, in the order asked; empty is "none stated") and `omitted` (the records that made it not whole: the parser's count per part, a refused record, a duplicate, one beyond the bound, plus each second copy across parts; 0 exactly when `complete`; absent on a single read that filled its limit, whose tail nobody can count). The count is over the RESPONSES the window was composed from, a month response whole: a record refused outside the span counts (it already makes `complete` false, and a refused record often has no readable date), so it bounds what the span may be missing, and every sentence built on it says "may be incomplete". Two season rules, kept two: the WINDOW states the season its stating parts agree on (a part that states none does not veto); DISCOVERY states one only when every month states the same. Two seasons in a window are then a failure when it is asked strictly (the default, and every caller that merges the bundle or keeps a slice: an absent season would let the bundle apply or a slice of another edition stand), and a composed answer with no `season` and both in `seasons` when it is asked `acrossSeasons`, which a caller that merges nothing and keeps no slice does (a refusal there was three requests spent on a verdict nobody can act on): the live read (`getLiveRead`: it keeps the matches in play), and OFF the bundled competition the dated read (`getMatchesForDate`: no skeleton to merge) and the match read (`match <id>` refreshing the one record it found). On the bundle the dated read on a turn day is still refused. A composed season keeps its start and end date only when every stating part (every month, for discovery) stated the same one (`agreedSeason`): agreeing on the year is not agreeing on when the edition ends. A fixture's day is the PROVIDER's (US/Eastern, measured; `bucketDay`), which is what the month filter keeps by and what the canary checks on every dated response. An incomplete answer cannot prove a fixture is gone: a caller that keeps a previous answer (the refresher's knockout slice, and off the bundle its schedule slice) must not let it erase one. It keeps what the answer did not READ (`mentioned`: everything the answer read, also a tie it set aside as postponed or played). Both rules below are about an INCOMPLETE answer; a whole answer that is taken replaces either slice (`applyDiscovery` refuses any answer, whole or not, whose index would pass its bound: that is a failed discovery, and the slice stands). The knockout slice (`runRefresh`), when it holds a tie that is upcoming (`isUpcoming`: not postponed or cancelled, its kickoff not yet passed): with both seasons known and the same (by year) it keeps the upcoming ties the answer did not read; with both known and different the answer replaces the slice; with either unknown the slice stands as it was and the read counts as a failed attempt. A knockout slice that holds no upcoming tie takes any answer that did not fail. What the cycle then publishes is still one season per snapshot: a knockout slice whose season is known to differ from the live read's is dropped. The schedule slice (`applyDiscovery`) keeps the entries at stake (kickoff from six hours ago, `RELEVANT_BACK_MS`, through 16 days ahead, `SCHEDULE_HORIZON_MS`, two days beyond discovery) that an incomplete answer did not read, unless the two seasons are known to DIFFER, because there an unknown season is an ordinary state (a discovery whose span touches two seasons states none); an incomplete answer known to be another season keeps none of them. Whatever an answer READ is taken from the answer: an entry it read and no longer returns inside the span is gone, whatever the seasons. The season the schedule slice then stores is a claim about every entry, so a union that KEPT entries stores the answer's season only when the two were already known to be the same, and none otherwise: a kept entry is never given a season it was not read under (an answer of "another" season could then delete it). The knockout-facing interactive reads (`next`, `bracket`, their share cards; CLI text and `--json`, MCP text and `data`) carry the window's `complete: false` as the `partial` verdict (see the verdict bullet), and so do `next` and `match <id>` off the bundle (`next` from discovery; `match <id>` from discovery when discovery did not hold the id, and from the match's own refresh alone when it did), and every other interactive read of fixtures: `today`, `live`, `match` on the bundle, the market surfaces (`markets <id>`, `markets next <team>` through `marketFixtureForTeam`, and `markets <date>`, which forwards the fixture read's verdict only where markets cover the competition, `marketsCoverCompetition`), and their MCP and share twins (see the verdict bullet for what each prints). The standings reads carry none of it: `getStandings` states a table's own `partial` and the inventory's `incomplete` (the table-key bullet). The statusline, the hook and the cache do not carry it: they are ambient and cache-only; the cache's live slice carries ONE boolean about its read, `liveComplete` (whether the read that filled it was whole), which the statusline's syncing rule, `ambient --json`'s `current` and its `finished` list ask, and no other verdict. The snapshot also keeps the refresher's record of play (`play`, `packages/cli/src/play.ts`: `seen` keeps an id for up to six hours after a whole read last saw it in play; `finished` keeps its match for up to ten minutes from the first whole read to confirm its finish; a snapshot is written without timelines and, if still over its size bound, `boundedSnapshot` clears `play.finished` first, `cache-bound.test.ts`), advanced by one pure function (`advancePlay`) under the lock on both publishers, given no transition by a failed, degraded or partial read (`advancePlay` then returns the previous record as it believes it at that moment: each of its two lists bounded, validated, and without a repeated id or an expired entry), carried by the refresher. Readers must accept a missing `play`. A truncated finished list confirms matches it holds and cannot tell whether an absent match is gone: full time is a match seen in play that a whole read returns finished, never a match that left the list; `ambient --json` lists it as `finished`, each entry the record still holds, for at most its own 10 minutes by the reader's clock, while the snapshot's last live read was whole (`liveComplete`); the snapshot's display window does not bound it. The reader (`finishedFromCache`) looks at the record's first 64 finished entries (`MAX_PLAY`) and lists none whose stamp is invalid or ahead of the clock, whose record does not seal as finished with a score, whose match the live list holds, or whose id it has already listed; the statusline's countdown and `ambient --json`'s `next` read only its ids (a match the record holds, in play or finished in its last 10 minutes, is never a due fixture's `now`; `playRecordIds`, once per render, without sealing a record), the hook reads none of it, and the hook's line gains `(FT)` for a finished record only (an English token, as the ambient surfaces are). A three-day window cut at the provider's limit still THROWS (it is `degraded`, not `partial`): turning it into a returned partial would change what the refresher does with it.
