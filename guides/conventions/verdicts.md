# Conventions: verdicts

This guide holds the narration behind the `AGENTS.md` "Conventions" bullet "A verdict becomes output in ONE place", moved out of `AGENTS.md` word for word. Where the narration continues a sentence `AGENTS.md` kept, or refers to one, the guide quotes that sentence or clause first. It also holds what `.cursor/rules/surface-parity.mdc` stated that no bullet does, under a heading naming the rule. Its last section holds a bullet whole, as it stood before `AGENTS.md` kept only its lead and the sentences an agent holds on every task. `AGENTS.md` stays canonical.

## A verdict becomes output in ONE place

(AGENTS.md keeps "A surface never forwards the field by hand at an emit site:") that is how `share --json` and `markets --json` each dropped a verdict their text carried, and how MCP `data` answered an unsupported competition exactly like an empty one.

## From `.cursor/rules/surface-parity.mdc`

### A verdict is forwarded by core, not by each surface

Pass the RESULT to them; do not name the verdict at an emit site (`packages/core/test/verdict.test.ts` fails when a surface's code spells `unsupported`, `noCompetition:`, `incomplete:`, `partial:`, `inapplicable:`, `unknownTeam:`, `rosterIncomplete:`, `betweenEditions:`, a verdict's sentence or i18n key, the horizon, window, "none read" or unserved sentence, a card's `emptyNote`/`installLine`, or a table's `partial` key). A table's structured form, with its `label` and its `partial` verdict, comes from `tableData`, and its unlocalized title from `tableTitle` (`packages/core/src/standings.ts`); a table argument is checked with `tableKeyArg` on every surface (the MCP tools check it through their input schema, which uses core's `TABLE_KEY_ARG`, the grammar `tableKeyArg` applies).

## The bullet whole: "A verdict becomes output in ONE place"

The `AGENTS.md` "Conventions" bullet as it stood on `main` before this section was added, word for word. `AGENTS.md` now keeps its lead and a selection of its sentences and clauses, with a few joining words and a closing sentence of its own. A rule stated only here still holds: a sentence `AGENTS.md` leaves out is not a rule withdrawn. A reference in this text to "above" or to another bullet is to `AGENTS.md`: the bullets before this one there, and for one of the four bullets held whole, its own guide.

**A verdict becomes output in ONE place.** When a result says something about itself that changes what a reader may conclude ("not available for this competition" is not "none found"), it states it as a field, and every surface turns that field into a structured key and a sentence through core (`packages/core/src/verdict.ts`): `verdictExtras` for the key, `verdictNotice` for the sentence that REPLACES the body, `verdictQualifiers` for the sentences that QUALIFY it. A surface never forwards the field by hand at an emit site: Adding a verdict means adding it there and declaring its key in the MCP output schemas (`verdictOut`; `incompleteOut` for the standings tools; `partialOut` for `get_today`, `get_live`, `get_match`, `get_next_fixture`, `get_bracket`, `get_market_signal` and `get_share_snippet`; in `packages/mcp/src/server.ts`; the strict schema test refuses an undeclared top-level key). A verdict that replaces the body (`verdictNotice`: six, the first stated in this order is said: `noCompetition`, nothing is chosen, stated by a selection (`selectionVerdict`), never by a read, and declared on every competition-answering MCP output schema, the standings tool's included; `unsupported`, not offered for this competition yet; `inapplicable`, the competition has no such thing, a bracket; `unknownTeam`, the roster the competition has holds no team by that name, with `rosterEvidence` saying which and the sentence naming it, never the competition: `bundle`, the World Cup's nations; `table`, the table read whole and a WHOLE read of the next 14 days' fixtures (a club out in a qualifying round is in no table, and an incomplete read is the partial empty body, never this claim); `rosterIncomplete`, the roster could not be read whole and the name could not be answered without it, which is not an outage; `betweenEditions: { ended, label? }`, the read's edition ended and nothing in it is current, `ended` being the provider's calendar day of the season end it STATES) is printed instead of it; one that qualifies what is shown (`verdictQualifiers`, in a fixed order: `incomplete`, tables are missing; `partial`, the read the result was made from was not whole (a knockout window, a dated or live window, discovery, a match's refresh), `partial: { omitted }` with the count of provider records left out when it is known, `partial: {}` when not) is printed with it, on a populated body and an empty one (an empty `next` beside `partial` is "none read", never "eliminated"), and FIRST where a text is cut at a length (a tool's answer, a share card), as a table's `partial` sentence is printed before its table (the standings sites keep the positions they had: CLI `table` after the tables, MCP before). When a result states a replacement, its qualifiers are not printed. On a surface a cut can reach (a tool's text, a share card) every sentence that qualifies the body is printed BEFORE it, and nothing follows the body but the footer: the verdict's qualifiers first, then the sentence counting the shown rows a read did not serve, then the surface's own notes (a degraded line, a market-incomplete notice, a list's truncation, a roster note, a bracket's note) in the order they used to follow the body (MCP `qualified(body, result, lang, ...also)` takes the sentences after the verdict's as a list; the standings tool and the share formatters keep the same order themselves). The CLI commands other than `share` are not cut and keep their notes after the body. A result states `partial` only when its read SAID it was not whole (`fetchMeta`: `complete: false`), never when the adapter said nothing, and a read that states it succeeded, so it is not what makes a result `degraded`; one result carries both: an off-bundle `match` whose refresh was not whole and did not hold the id is `partial` (the refresh was) AND `degraded` (the record shown is the provider's earlier one); the count is believed only as a positive integer, and the one rule that turns a read's account into the verdict is `partialOfRead`; the cut drops the end of the body, never the tool's footer (the attribution and the disclaimer: `ToolResult.footer`, kept by `toContent`), except that a DATE's list (`get_today`, the date card) on a read that was not whole keeps only the disclaimer (`ToolResult.cutFooter`): the rows a cut drops may be every row the provider served, so the attribution line goes with them while the structured `source` keeps the provider; a live list keeps its line after any cut (every row it shows was served); every emit site of a result that can state one already passes that result through, and a result type that gains its first verdict is wired once, where it is emitted. A table's structured form, with its `partial` verdict, has one mapping too (`tableData` in `packages/core/src/standings.ts`). The share cards that `claudinho share` and `get_share_snippet` hand out are assembled once, in `packages/core/src/share/cards.ts` (title, empty note, run cue, attribution, verdict, and `note`: the qualifying sentences, which the share formatters print before the body whenever it is set, followed by the card's own notes, also before the body); a surface serializes the card and, for MCP, bounds its list. `packages/core/test/verdict.test.ts` guards this by vocabulary (a surface's code never spells `unsupported`, `noCompetition:`, `incomplete:`, `partial:`, `inapplicable:`, `unknownTeam:`, `rosterIncomplete:` or `betweenEditions:` outside the MCP schema, a verdict's sentence or i18n key, the horizon, window, "none read" or unserved sentence, a card's empty note or run cue, or a table's `partial` key); it catches a copy, not every way to write one, so the rule is still yours to keep. The empty-body sentences off the bundle are NOT verdicts: `next`'s horizon (`horizon: { days }`, "no fixture for X within the next 14 days") and `match <id>`'s window (`window: { from, to }`, "not found between A and B") after a whole read, and "none read" after one that was not whole ("no fixture for X was read in this span", "no match with id N was read in this span", under the `partial` sentence) are the answer's own text, built once (`nextHorizonSentence`, `matchWindowSentence`, `nextNoneReadSentence`, `matchNoneReadSentence` in `share/cards.ts`), with the span as a plain field; a verdict replaces them, and they never suppress a qualifier. So is the pointer an empty `today` or `live` off the bundle carries: the next local day with a fixture that is on (not postponed, cancelled or finished), its count only when that whole day lies in the span read and, on the CLI alone, the `claudinho today` command that shows it (`nextDay: { date, count? }`); for `live` the next kickoff AHEAD, never a match now (`nextKickoff`), and nothing extra while the schedule ahead holds a match in play (within `IN_PLAY_HOLD_MS`, six hours, of its kickoff) or one inside its live window (`entryInLiveWindow`, the refresher's gate's rule); `horizon: { days: 14 }` when nothing that is on falls in the span (for a day, only when the day shown is today). It is decided once in `packages/core/src/ahead.ts` (`pointerWanted`, the gate, with `nextDayReachable` for a day, asked before any extra read; `dayPointer`, `livePointer`; `pointerExtras`, the plain fields' one mapping) and said once in `share/cards.ts` (`nextDayLines`, `nextKickoffLines`, `todayCommand`), read only on an empty body after a read that did not fail and was whole, with no verdict replacing it, from the schedule ahead read whole (`readAhead`), never under a cooldown, the CLI from its schedule slice first when that is whole, its index intact (`indexIntact`) and fresh (written on the request's provider day within the discovery TTL), and only for days it still covers (`dayCovered`: the refresher drops fixtures six hours old), else one fresh read, never under a backoff in effect (`backoffInEffect`), never writing it. The same holds on the bundle and for the live and dated reads: an empty body after a read that was not whole says nothing was READ, never that nothing exists (`live`: "no match in play was read"; a day that merged no bundled schedule, off the bundle or the bundle's slug answering for another edition: "no fixture was read for D"; a dated market answer: "no market signal among the fixtures read for D", which is market copy from the approved bank, English on every locale; `liveNoneRead`, `dateNoneRead`, `marketsNoneRead`, each built on its `...Sentence`). A day whose read merged the bundled schedule stays whole and keeps the bundle's empty answer. A day whose read FAILED and merged none (off the bundle) says the provider could not be reached ("couldn't reach the data provider — no fixtures confirmed for D", `dateUnreached`, the date card's sentence on every date surface), and its degraded line says the same, never "showing the bundled schedule", which only the bundle's degraded day says. The dated read says what it is made of in two request-local fields, never cached: `skeleton` (the bundled schedule was merged under the records) and `served` (the ids the window held); the bundled `match <id>` read states `served` too. `served` rides in the structured twins as a plain field beside `source`, not a verdict, only BESIDE `partial`, the verdict it interprets, and bounded like the rows it interprets: the SHOWN fixtures whose record the window held (the day's rows, MCP's bounded list, the card's matches, the one match), never the window's every id. One rule (`servedExtras(result, shown)`, beside `verdictExtras`) at every site that carries it (`today --json`, `share <date>`/`<id> --json`, `match --json`, MCP `get_today`, `get_match` and `get_share_snippet` `data`), so a whole read's structured output keeps its shape; the results themselves state the window's ids on every successful read. On a read that was NOT whole a day's surface decides its attribution from them over the fixtures it finally DISPLAYS (after the date filter and, on MCP, the bound), through ONE rule (`dayAttribution`): displayed rows the window did not serve are counted in a second qualifying sentence ("N fixture(s) shown from the bundled schedule; their live state is unconfirmed", after the `partial` one), and when NONE of the displayed fixtures was served the "Live data" line is not printed (CLI text, the MCP text's footer, the date card, whose `source` then is null) while CLI `--json` `source` and MCP `data.source` keep the overlay's provider. The bundled `match <id>` (CLI, MCP, its card) asks the same rule of the one record it shows: a record its window did not hold is named as the bundle's. A whole read changes nothing; `live` never shows a row its read did not serve. The market surfaces print the fixture read's qualifiers beside the market body and carry its key, apart from `complete`/`marketComplete`, which describe the market requests; `marketFixtureForTeam` states the verdict of BOTH its reads (the knockout window, and the candidate's own day when it refreshed one), the counts summed when every stating read knew its count, over the responses like the window's own count (a record both refused counts in each): an upper bound on the records left out; the dated market answer forwards its dated read's verdict where markets are read.
